## How to plan a holiday in Japan

Japan brings together contemporary cities, historic temples, ryokans, hot springs and a food culture attentive to every detail. A well-paced itinerary reveals different sides of the country without turning the holiday into a succession of transfers. The right route depends on the season, the length of the trip and each traveller’s interests.

### When to go

Spring draws visitors for cherry blossoms, while autumn brings colourful foliage and pleasant conditions for exploring. Summer offers festivals and mountain escapes; winter is suited to skiing, hot springs and snow-covered scenery. Weather varies considerably from north to south, and the timing of blossoms and autumn colours changes each year. Popular travel dates and sought-after accommodation should be planned early.

### A first itinerary

For a first visit, Tokyo and Kyoto make a natural combination, with a few nights in a ryokan in Hakone or another hot-spring region. A longer journey can include Osaka, Nara or Kanazawa. Allow time for more than landmark visits: quiet walks, memorable meals and unplanned moments give the trip its character.

### Cities or regions

Tokyo offers contemporary architecture, design-led hotels, shopping and exceptional dining. Kyoto is known for temples, gardens, traditional neighbourhoods and tea culture. Hakone provides a change of pace amid natural scenery and hot springs. Travellers returning to Japan can explore less-visited regions, choosing according to the season, their interests and the time available.

### Ryokan and onsen

A ryokan stay is an experience in its own right: traditional interiors, attentive hospitality and a seasonal dinner create a different rhythm. Facilities vary between properties. Not every ryokan has a private onsen, and the rules for shared baths should be checked before booking. Travellers seeking more privacy can choose a room with its own bathing facilities or reserve a private bathing session where available.

### Choosing accommodation

In major cities, location and access to daily plans matter; in a ryokan, consider the room category, dinner, views and bathing arrangements. Families should check room size and policies for children. Fine-dining restaurants, popular ryokans and specialist cultural experiences are best arranged ahead of time, especially in peak seasons.

### Transfers and luggage

High-speed trains make journeys between major cities convenient, but frequent hotel changes and connections can make a trip feel rushed. Luggage-forwarding services between hotels make it easier to travel with a small overnight bag for a short stop. Thoughtful planning balances transport, accommodation and time to enjoy each place.
