Ski holidays in the Alps: how to choose the right resort

The Alps bring together some of the most renowned ski resorts in France, Switzerland and Italy, ranging from extensive interconnected ski areas to atmospheric Alpine villages. A winter holiday here can include a five-star hotel or a private chalet, ski-in/ski-out accommodation, slopes for different ability levels, fine dining, wellness facilities and a lively après-ski scene.

French Alps: Courchevel, Megève, Val d’Isère and Chamonix

Courchevel is known for its access to Les 3 Vallées, exceptional hotels and chalets, restaurants and sophisticated resort infrastructure. Megève combines skiing with the charm of a traditional Alpine village, excellent gastronomy and a more relaxed pace. Val d’Isère is particularly suited to travellers looking for varied terrain and an active skiing experience, while Chamonix appeals to those drawn to dramatic high-mountain scenery, a strong sporting character and the setting beneath Mont Blanc.

Swiss Alps: St. Moritz, Gstaad, Zermatt and Verbier

Swiss resorts offer distinctly different styles of winter travel. St. Moritz combines skiing with legendary hotels and a vibrant social scene. Gstaad is celebrated for its chalets, refined atmosphere and high level of service. Zermatt offers skiing beneath the Matterhorn and the distinctive experience of a resort largely free from conventional car traffic. Verbier is a strong choice for active skiers, freeride enthusiasts and travellers who enjoy a lively après-ski atmosphere.

Italian Alps: Cortina d’Ampezzo and Alta Badia

In the Dolomites, skiing is closely connected with Italian gastronomy and lifestyle. Cortina d’Ampezzo combines striking mountain scenery with high-end hotels, restaurants and an active resort atmosphere. Alta Badia is known for its scenic Dolomite slopes, excellent cuisine and a more relaxed style of mountain holiday.

Hotels, chalets and tailor-made holidays in the Alps

Choosing the right Alpine resort involves more than the country or the quality of the skiing. The location of a hotel or chalet in relation to the lifts, ski-in/ski-out access, the composition of the travelling party, facilities for children, dining, wellness, transfers and the preferred pace of the holiday can all influence the experience.

The same region can work equally well for an intensive week on the slopes or for a family journey combining comfortable skiing, gastronomy and spa time. An Alpine itinerary can also include several resorts or be combined with a few days in a European city. The destination, accommodation and logistics are best planned as one coherent journey, taking into account the season, skiing ability and individual travel preferences.
